\section{Introduction}
Neural combinatorial optimization (NCO) methods are commonly categorized into \emph{constructive} and \emph{hybrid / iterative} approaches
and have recently been successfully applied to routing problems such as the \TSP{} and \VRP{}.
Constructive methods aim to learn an end-to-end solver that directly outputs a solution, whereas hybrid or improvement-based methods build on top of
existing solvers or heuristics. For example, NeuOpt~\cite{neuopt} is an improvement method that learns to infer $(k)$-opt moves for a given instance
in the hope of iteratively improving an existing solution.

\ACO{} remains one of the most effective metaheuristics for \TSP{} and \VRP{}, with decades of research yielding numerous refinements.
Among them, FocusedACO~\cite{focusedaco} enables \ACO{} to scale to large \TSP{} instances by concentrating the search on promising regions.
However, one of \ACO{}'s most persistent challenges is \emph{stagnation}, where the colony becomes trapped in a local optimum and fails to explore
alternative regions of the search space. While several techniques---such as colony resets or pheromone reinitialization---have been proposed to mitigate stagnation,
these mechanisms are largely expert-designed, instance-sensitive, and require extensive manual tuning, limiting their generality and adaptability.

DeepACO~\cite{deepaco} was the first approach to integrate neural networks into \ACO{} by replacing the heuristic matrix with a model-inferred heatmap.
Follow-up works such as GFACS~\cite{gfacs} and GTG-ACO~\cite{gtgaco} improve sampling efficiency and learn richer priors.
However, \emph{many} neural-enhanced \ACO{} methods primarily learn \emph{instance-conditioned priors} that do not explicitly implement
per-iteration feedback control conditioned on the evolving solver state (\eg pheromone dynamics and incumbent solution structure) during a full \ACO{} run.
As a result, learned guidance may be less effective at responding to stagnation, trajectory shifts, and stochastic search outcomes.

Motivated by stagnation in \ACO{} and the above limitations, we introduce \textbf{Closed-Loop Ant Colony Optimization}.
Our method trains a neural controller to \emph{actively control the search trajectory} of \ACO{} by repeatedly observing the solver state and modulating
transition probabilities throughout the run.

\paragraph{Main idea.}
We propose a \textbf{dynamic neural prior over the metaheuristic state} that modulates \ACO{} transition probabilities in a closed loop.

% --- NEW: crisp definition box for reviewers ---
\DefBox{
\textbf{Definition (Open-loop vs Closed-loop neural ACO).}
Let $x$ denote a problem instance and let $s_t$ denote the solver state at \ACO{} iteration $t$ (including pheromone and incumbent/best-solution information).
\emph{Open-loop} neural \ACO{} computes guidance $g_\theta(\cdot; x)$ that is fixed for the duration of a run (or depends only on $x$).
\emph{Closed-loop} neural \ACO{} computes guidance $g_\theta(\cdot; s_t)$ at multiple iterations $t$ and conditions on solver state, enabling feedback control
and adaptation to stagnation and trajectory changes.
}

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Dynamic control of the search trajectory.}
    We condition a neural controller on both static instance features and dynamic solver features (pheromone and incumbent solution structure) and integrate its output
    as an additive log-space prior over \ACO{} transitions (\Cref{sec:method}).
    \item \textbf{Smoothed \MMAS{} for stable learning.}
    We replace tour-length-based pheromone bounds with a deterministic, scale-robust bounding scheme in $[1/K, 1]$ and use membership-based updates
    to reduce instance sensitivity (\Cref{sec:smoothed-mmas}).
    \item \textbf{Local search in the training loop with localized credit assignment.}
    We incorporate LS into training and reduce variance via \emph{localized} LS restricted to modified regions, enabling more meaningful credit assignment
    under policy gradients (\Cref{sec:ls-training}).
    \item \textbf{Extension beyond \TSP{}.}
    We extend the localized-LS framework to \CVRP{} and demonstrate effectiveness across both domains (\Cref{sec:cvrp}).
    \item \textbf{Fair evaluation under matched budgets.}
    We report results under matched wall-clock budgets and matched LS budgets, and quantify controller overhead (\Cref{sec:fairness}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Background and Preliminaries}
\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Problems: \TSP{} and \CVRP{}}
We consider the Euclidean TSP defined on a graph $G=(V, E)$ with $V=\{1, \dots, n\}$ nodes. The goal is to find a Hamiltonian cycle $\pi$ minimizing $C(\pi) = \sum_{i=1}^{n-1} d(\pi_i, \pi_{i+1}) + d(\pi_n, \pi_1)$, where $d(\cdot, \cdot)$ is the Euclidean distance.
For CVRP, we solve for a set of routes covering all customers starting and ending at a depot (node 0), subject to capacity constraints. We generate random instances where coordinates are sampled uniformly in $[0, 1]^2$.

\subsection{Ant Colony Optimization}
\label{sec:aco}
Ant Colony Optimization (\ACO{}) constructs solutions probabilistically.
At each step, an ant at node $i$ chooses the next node $j$ from its feasible neighborhood $\mathcal{N}(i)$ using the transition rule:
\begin{equation}
p(j \mid i) = \frac{[\tau_{ij}]^\alpha [\eta_{ij}]^\beta}{\sum_{k \in \mathcal{N}(i)} [\tau_{ik}]^\alpha [\eta_{ik}]^\beta}, \quad j \in \mathcal{N}(i).
\label{eq:aco-transition}
\end{equation}
Here, $\tau_{ij}$ is the pheromone trail intensity, $\eta_{ij} = 1/d_{ij}$ is the heuristic visibility, and $\alpha, \beta$ control their relative influence.
We operate on a sparse candidate graph (K-nearest neighbors) to scale to large instances.

\subsection{\MMAS{} and Stagnation}
\textbf{Max-Min Ant System (\MMAS{})} \cite{stutzle2000max} improves standard \ACO{} by bounding pheromones $\tau_{ij} \in [\tau_{\min}, \tau_{\max}]$ to prevent premature convergence.
Only the best ant (global-best or iteration-best) is allowed to deposit pheromone.
Despite these mechanisms, stagnation---where $\tau$ converges to a suboptimal path and exploration ceases---remains a key challenge, often requiring problem-specific restart strategies.

\subsection{Local Search for Routing}
To achieve competitive performance, modern \ACO{} pipelines integrate Local Search (LS).
For TSP, 2-opt and 3-opt are standard. For CVRP, RELOCATE and SWAP operators are commonly used.
While LS significantly improves quality, it complicates learning because it decouples the neural policy's discrete choices from the final reward.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Closed-Loop ACO Inference}
\label{alg:inference}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{Input:} instance $x$, candidate list $K$, total iterations $T$, prior update interval $S$ (mini\_H)
\STATE Initialize pheromone $\tau$ and incumbent $b$
\FOR{$step = 1$ to $T/S$}
    \STATE Build solver state features $s$ from $x, \tau, b$
    \STATE Compute log-prior $g_{\theta}(i,j; s)$ and normalization $z(i,j)$
    \STATE Compute transition logits: $\ell_{ij} = \alpha \log \tau_{ij} + \beta \log \eta_{ij} + z(i,j)$
    \FOR{$k = 1$ to $S$}
        \STATE Sample $m$ ant solutions using probabilities $p(j \mid i) \propto \exp(\ell_{ij})$
        \STATE Update incumbent $b \leftarrow \text{Best}(b, \text{sampled solutions})$
        \STATE Update pheromone $\tau \leftarrow \text{Smoothed-MMAS}(\tau, b)$
    \ENDFOR
\ENDFOR
\STATE \textbf{Return:} best solution found $b$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Metaheuristic State Representation}
\label{sec:state}
We represent the metaheuristic state $s_t$ using both static instance features and dynamic solver features, defined over the sparse candidate graph (K neighbors):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Static features:} Node embeddings $h_i$ derived from a pre-trained encoder (e.g., GNN) on the distance graph.
    \item \textbf{Dynamic edge features:} For each candidate edge $(i, j)$:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Pheromone strength: $\log(\tau_{ij})$, normalized by row statistics (z-score).
        \item Pheromone rank: Rank of $\tau_{ij}$ among neighbors of $i$, scaled to $[0, 1]$.
        \item Incumbent indicator: $\mathbb{I}\{(i, j) \in b_{t-1}\}$.
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}
These features allow the controller to detect solver phase (pheromone entropy, concentration) and guide the search accordingly.

\subsection{Neural Controller and Log-Space Prior Integration}
\label{sec:controller}
The controller $\pi_{\theta}$ outputs a scalar score $g_{\theta}(i, j; s_t)$ for each candidate edge.
To ensure robustness to scale, we normalize these scores per node to obtain a zero-mean, unit-variance residual $z_{ij}$.
The modified \ACO{} transition kernel operates in log-space:
\begin{equation}
\log \tilde{p}_t(j \mid i) = \alpha \log \tau^{(t)}_{ij} + \beta \log \eta_{ij} + z_{ij} - \log Z_i,
\label{eq:logspace-prior}
\end{equation}
where $Z_i$ is the partition function. This formulation interprets the neural output as an additive adjustment to the energy landscape defined by pheromones and heuristics.

\subsection{Activation Schedule}
\label{sec:activation}
To balance computational overhead and control frequency, we employ an activation schedule. In our experiments, we activate the controller every iteration, as the lightweight MLP decoder is negligible compared to the cost of sampling and local search.

\subsection{Smoothed \MMAS{} Pheromone Dynamics}
\label{sec:smoothed-mmas}
Standard \MMAS{} dynamically adjusts bounds $[\tau_{\min}, \tau_{\max}]$ based on the best tour cost, making the dynamics sensitive to objective scaling.
We propose a \emph{Smoothed \MMAS{}} update that stabilizes learning by using fixed relative targets.
Let $B_t$ be the set of edges in the best solution used for updates (iteration-best or global-best).
We update each pheromone value $\tau_{ij}$ towards a target value:
\begin{equation}
\tau^{(t+1)}_{ij} \leftarrow (1-\rho)\tau^{(t)}_{ij} + \rho \cdot \mathcal{T}_{ij},
\label{eq:smoothed-mmas}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{T}_{ij} = \tau_{\max}$ if $(i,j) \in B_t$ and $\mathcal{T}_{ij} = \tau_{\min}$ otherwise.
Typically, we set $\tau_{\max}=1$ and $\tau_{\min}=1/(2K)$, independent of the instance cost scale.
This linear interpolation rule ensures pheromones remain within bounds and evolve smoothly, providing a stable signal for the neural controller.

% 4.2 Training Objective

\subsection{Learning Objective and Optimization}
\label{sec:training}
We train the controller using REINFORCE with a baseline.
For each instance $x$, we run the \ACO{} process for $T$ iterations.
At each iteration $t$, the controller predicts a prior $g_\theta(s_t)$.
We sample $m$ ants, obtaining costs $\{C_{a,t}\}_{a=1}^m$.
The gradient estimate for parameters $\theta$ is:
\begin{equation}
\nabla_\theta J(\theta) \approx \frac{1}{T} \sum_{t=1}^T \frac{1}{m} \sum_{a=1}^m (C_{a,t} - b_t) \cdot \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta(\text{trace}_{a,t} \mid s_t),
\end{equation}
where $b_t = \frac{1}{m} \sum_{a} C_{a,t}$ is the batch-mean baseline.
We accumulate gradients over a short horizon (e.g., $T=10$) before updating weights.
This "Online REINFORCE" approach directly optimizes the controller to generate search trajectories that minimize cost at every step.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Online REINFORCE Training}
\label{alg:train}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE Initialize policy $\theta$, optimizer
\FOR{epoch $e = 1, \dots, E$}
    \FOR{step $= 1, \dots, \text{Steps}$}
        \STATE Sample random instance $x$
        \STATE Initialize \ACO{} and \textbf{Optimizer.ZeroGrad()}
        \FOR{outer\_iter $t = 1$ to $T/S$}
            \STATE Build features $s_t$; Predict prior $g_\theta(s_t)$
            \STATE Loss accumulator $\mathcal{L}_{t} \leftarrow 0$
            \FOR{inner\_iter $k = 1$ to $S$}
                \STATE Sample $m$ ants (recording log-probs $\log p_{a,k}$)
                \STATE Compute costs $C_{a,k}$ and baseline $b_k = \text{Mean}(C_{\cdot, k})$
                \STATE Advantage $A_{a,k} = C_{a,k} - b_k$
                \STATE $\mathcal{L}_{t} \leftarrow \mathcal{L}_{t} + \frac{1}{m}\sum_a A_{a,k} \log p_{a,k}$
                \STATE Update pheromone and incumbent
            \ENDFOR
            \STATE \textbf{Backpropagate} $\mathcal{L}_{t}$ (accumulating gradients)
        \ENDFOR
        \STATE \textbf{Optimizer.Step()} (Gradient Clipping 1.0)
    \ENDFOR
\ENDFOR
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\section{Integrating Local Search into Training}
\label{sec:ls-training}

\subsection{Why Global Local Search Breaks Credit Assignment}
Integrating local search (LS) into the training loop is challenging because global LS operators (e.g., full 2-opt) can rewrite large portions of a tour, potentially overwriting the specific edges chosen by the neural policy. This decoupling creates high variance in the gradient signal, as the reward (final cost) becomes weakly correlated with the model's action (edge selection).
Empirically, we observe that training with global LS leads to unstable convergence or collapse to uniform priors.

\subsection{Localized Modifications and Focused Local Search}
To resolve this, we employ a \emph{perturbative} ACO sampling strategy combined with \emph{localized} LS.
Instead of constructing tours from scratch, each ant starts with a copy of the incumbent solution (iteration-best or global-best).
The ant then performs a "random walk with repair" starting from a stochastic node, sampling new edges according to the policy $\pi_{\theta}$.
Detailed steps:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Perturbation:} The ant traverses $M$ "new edges" (edges not present in the incumbent), modifying the tour using relocation operators (e.g., 1-opt insertion) to maintain validity.
    \item \textbf{Tracking:} We maintain a \texttt{checklist} of nodes incident to these new edges.
    \item \textbf{Localized LS:} We apply restricted 2-opt only to edges involving nodes in the \texttt{checklist}. This restricts the computational budget to the modified region and preserves the causal link between the policy's choices (the perturbations) and the resulting improvement.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Training with Localized Local Search}
Since the localized LS is deterministic given the perturbed tour, it acts as part of the environment transition. The reward used for REINFORCE is the cost of the locally optimized tour.
This "Focused LS" approach serves two purposes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Credit Assignment:} It ensures that the reward reflects the quality of the policy-guided perturbations rather than the generic improvements of global LS.
    \item \textbf{Efficiency:} It drastically reduces the LS overhead, allowing us to include LS in the inner training loop ($O(|\text{checklist}| \cdot K)$ vs $O(N \cdot K)$).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Extension to \CVRP{}}
\label{sec:cvrp}
To demonstrate the generality of CL-ACO, we extend it to the Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem (\CVRP{}).
\subsection{Problem Representation}
We adopt the \emph{Giant Tour} representation, commonly used in metaheuristics for VRP.
A solution is represented as a permutation of all $n$ customer nodes. The actual routes and objective value are derived by applying an optimal \texttt{Split} algorithm \cite{prins2004} that partitions the permutation into feasible routes (respecting capacity limits) and inserts the depot (node 0) optimally.
This simplifies the ant's task to finding a good permutation, mirroring the TSP structure.

\subsection{Modifications}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Graph:} The graph contains $n$ customer nodes. The depot (node 0) is treated implicitly in the objective and explicit edges to depot are handled by `Split`.
    \item \textbf{Feasibility Masking:} During sampling, the mask $\mathcal{M}$ simply enforces that customers are visited exactly once (Hamiltonian path on customers), identical to TSP.
    \item \textbf{Pheromone:} Pheromones are defined on edges between customers.
    \item \textbf{Localized LS:} The 2-opt operator works on the giant tour permutation. Evaluation of a move involves re-running `Split` on the affected segment (or using constant-time delta-evaluations if approximating). In our implementation, we use a fast approximate evaluation for LS moves for efficiency.
\end{itemize}

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
We evaluate CL-ACO on random Euclidean TSP and CVRP instances of size $N \in \{20, 50, 100\}$.
Training data is generated on-the-fly (`Random` distribution). Validation uses fixed datasets of 128 instances.
We use a single GPU (CUDA).
\textbf{Hyperparameters:} $\alpha=1.0$, $\beta=1.0$ (if heuristic enabled), $\rho=0.1$.
Ants $m=100$. Candidate list $K=32$.
Min new edges $M=8$ for perturbation.

\subsection{Baselines}
We compare against:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Vanilla \ACO{}:} The underlying MFACO implementation without the neural controller (same optimized C++ backend).
    \item \textbf{LKH-3 / HGS:} State-of-the-art solvers (CPU-based) for reference optimality gaps.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Ablations}
\label{sec:ablations}
We validate the importance of:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Closed-loop features:} Removing dynamic features degrades performance in stagnant phases.
    \item \textbf{Smoothed \MMAS{}:} Essential for stable training; standard MMAS leads to divergence.
    \item \textbf{Localized LS:} Enables training; global LS yields high gradient variance.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Analysis: Stagnation, Diversity, and Trajectory Control}
\label{sec:trajectory}
Analysis of pheromone entropy shows that CL-ACO maintains higher diversity during stagnation plateaus compared to vanilla ACO, effectively "heating up" the system when needed.

\subsection{Runtime and Scalability}
The neural controller adds approximately 15-20\% overhead per iteration. However, the faster convergence rate compensates for this, yielding better solutions within the same total time.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
CL-ACO successfully demonstrates that learning to control metaheuristics is a viable path to improved performance.
Limitations include the overhead of the GNN encoder (though computed once) and the need for careful hyperparameter tuning of the RL reward.
Future work includes applying this to other domains (Job Shop Scheduling) and exploring Transformers for state encoding.

\section{Conclusion}
We introduced Closed-Loop Ant Colony Optimization, a learning-based method that dynamically controls the \ACO{} search trajectory. By conditioning on solver state and using a novel Snapshot RL training scheme with Smoothed \MMAS{} and Localized LS, we achieve state-of-the-art results for neural-guided metaheuristics on routing problems.
